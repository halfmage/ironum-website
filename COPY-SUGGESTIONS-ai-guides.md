## Executive Summary

The body copy came over from LearnSlice in good shape. It is evidence-led, restrained,
and gives the knowledge away before asking for anything. That is right for product people,
who discount marketing language on reflex. The article is the strongest piece: it opens on
a moment every PO recognises ("You asked Copilot what the team agreed about an epic, and it
told you nothing useful") and earns its CTAs.

Most of the lost points come from how the pages were assembled on Ironum, not from the
writing:

1. **No CTA above the fold.** The shared dark hero holds only the title and the tagline.
   On a laptop, the first "Get the guide" button sits below a hero half a screen tall.
2. **The commercial bridge is under-sold.** "When the limit is the tool, not you" is the
   reason these guides belong on Ironum, but it ends in a quiet inline link ("tell us which
   one"). The PDF already has a much stronger promise that the page leaves out: *"Alesia
   replies herself, within two working days, with whether it is solvable and roughly what it
   would take. No sales sequence follows."*
3. **The PO page opens on a German statistic for a US + EU audience.** "27% of German
   workplaces…" is strong for DACH but foreign to a US product owner. The universal hook
   (Copilot can't read your Jira) sits in the third section.

The hub is thin: two cards and one line. It needs a reason to choose between the guides.

## Value Proposition Analysis

| Element | Status |
|---|---|
| Target customer | **Clear.** Product managers and product owners in Microsoft 365 organisations |
| Problem | **Clear.** AI made me faster, not better / Copilot can't see my work |
| Solution | **Clear.** Role-specific, sourced, apply-this-week guides |
| Unique mechanism | **Weak on the page.** That the author has done the job for 17+ years, and that every figure is traced, is stated in small grey type |
| Key benefit | **Implicit.** "Better product decisions with the tools you already have" is never said outright |
| Proof | **Present** for the facts, **missing** for the guide itself (no cover preview, no reader quote, no "what you will be able to do after") |

The Ironum value proposition (AI that can read your Jira and Confluence) has the same
gap: the page names it but never shows the outcome. The PDF's own sentence does show it and
should be reused: *"check every item against the Product Goal, find duplicates across the
whole backlog rather than the twenty items you pasted."* The PO page already includes it.
The PM page needs its equivalent.

## Headline Recommendations

The H1s (`AI for Product Owners`, `AI for Product Managers`) carry the keyword and should
stay. The SEO audit depends on them. The work happens in the subheadline, which is what
people actually read.

### PO subheadline
**Current:** "Where it fits your sprint, and where it stops."

Ten alternatives, ranked:
1. **"Your Copilot probably can't read your backlog. Here is what AI can still do for refinement, and where it stops."** (PAS, universal hook)
2. "Sharper refinement, testable acceptance criteria, and the one part of the job no tool can take." (4U)
3. "What AI can do for refinement, ordering and sprint goals, and what stays yours." (clarity)
4. "From pasting tickets into Copilot and hoping, to a method that works on any licence." (Before-After-Bridge)
5. "Where it fits your sprint, and where it stops. A free 21-page guide, every figure sourced." (current + specificity)
6. "The connector that works and still returns nothing, and the prompts worth keeping." (curiosity, from the PDF cover)
7. "Most product owners were never taught this. It takes thirty minutes a week." (AIDA)
8. "Refinement goes faster when the obvious questions are answered before the meeting." (benefit)
9. "Nine in ten workplaces use free AI tools. Barely a quarter train anyone in them." (stat; better for the DE version)
10. "AI for product owners, written by one." (identity; strong but needs the byline right next to it)

### PM subheadline
**Current:** "Where it changes the product, not just the pace."
1. **"97% of product managers say AI made them faster. Only 64% say it made the product better. Here is how to close that gap."**: move the best hook into the hero
2. "Where it changes the product, not just the pace." (current; keep as the eyebrow or the second line)
3. "Five places in a product week where AI is worth the time, and what it cannot do in each."

### Hub H1
**Current:** "Practical AI Guides for Product Teams"
1. **"AI guides for product people, written by one"**
2. "Free AI guides for product managers and product owners"
3. "Use the AI you already have, properly"

## Section-by-Section Copy Suggestions

### Hero (both landing pages)
**Issue:** no CTA, and the shared hero is half a screen tall with only a title in it.
**Fix:** pass `ctaText="Get the free guide"` and `ctaLink="#get-the-guide"` to `PageHero`.
The component currently shows Gerrit's avatar inside every CTA button, so it needs a
`showAvatar={false}` option first, otherwise the button reads "Book call with Gerrit"
visually. Alternatively, set `dark={false}` for a shorter light hero.

### PO opening statistic
**Before:**
> Of German workplaces already using generative AI, 27% give their people any training in it. Only 21% have written down any rules for using it.

**After:**
> If your backlog lives in Jira or Azure DevOps, no version of Microsoft Copilot reads it on its own. Most product owners find that out the hard way, after deciding the tool is overrated.
>
> In Germany, where it has been measured, only 27% of workplaces using generative AI train anyone in it. Nobody taught you this either. The guide does.

**Why:** it opens on the reader's own moment, which works in the US and the EU alike. The
German figure becomes evidence instead of the headline. It also removes the cannibalisation
risk the SEO audit flagged: the Jira hook moves into the intro, and the page's Jira H2 can be
shortened.

### Byline (both pages + hub)
**Before:**
> Written by **Alesia Kunz**, Product Manager at Ironum and CEO of LearnSlice, the company behind Ironum, after 17+ years in software engineering as a product manager and product owner.

**After:**
> Written by **[Alesia Kunz](/about/)**, who has spent 17+ years doing this job as a product manager and product owner. She is Product Manager at Ironum and CEO of LearnSlice, the company behind it.

**Why:** "Ironum" appeared twice in one clause and the credential that matters (17 years in
the role) came last. The link also helps E-E-A-T (SEO audit, item 7).

### Sources line
**Before:** "Figures come from the IAB establishment panel, Eurostat, the OECD, the Scrum Guide and Microsoft's own documentation."
**After:** "Every figure is traced to whoever published it: Germany's federal employment research institute (IAB), Eurostat, the OECD, the Scrum Guide and Microsoft's own documentation. Nothing is estimated."

### "When the limit is the tool, not you" (both pages)
**Before (end of section):**
> If you have hit one of these limits, tell us which one.

**After:**
> If you have hit one of these limits, write to Alesia with the limit and the system your backlog lives in. She replies herself, within two working days, with whether it is solvable and roughly what it would take. No sales sequence follows.
>
> **[Ask whether your limit is solvable →]** (button → `/contact/?source=guide-po`)

**Why:** it reuses the PDF's own promise word for word, so page and guide agree. It gives a
concrete next step with a stated cost (two days, no sequence), and it is the only
commercial CTA on the page. It stays honest with the form copy ("we will not contact you
unless you write to us").

### PM page: show the outcome
Add one sentence to the bridge section, the PM equivalent of the PO's backlog sentence:
> When a model can read your research notes, tickets and metrics directly, it can answer "what did customers actually say about onboarding this quarter" across all of it, not the three interviews you pasted, and cite where each answer came from.

### Form section heading
**Before:** "Get the guide" (H2)
**After:** "Download the free AI guide for product owners" / "…for product managers"
(Also an SEO fix.)

### FAQ and related headings
**Before:** "Common Questions" / "Related"
**After:** "AI for product owners: common questions" / "More on AI and your backlog"

### Hub body
Add a short "Which one is for me?" line under the cards:
> Product owners get the guide built around refinement and ordering. Everyone else, including heads of product, gets the product manager guide, which has a section on leading a team.

That logic already existed in LearnSlice's form routing and was lost when the pages were split.

## Before/After Examples

1. **PM hero subheadline**
   - BEFORE: "Where it changes the product, not just the pace."
   - AFTER: "97% of product managers say AI made them faster. Only 64% say it made the product better. Here is how to close that gap."
   - WHY: the strongest proof in the cluster sits in paragraph one; move it into the area people read first.

2. **PO intro**: see "PO opening statistic" above.

3. **Bridge CTA**
   - BEFORE: "tell us which one" (inline link)
   - AFTER: button "Ask whether your limit is solvable", plus the two-working-days promise
   - WHY: specific next step, stated cost, matches the PDF.

4. **Hub H1**
   - BEFORE: "Practical AI Guides for Product Teams"
   - AFTER: "AI guides for product people, written by one"
   - WHY: "practical" is a claim every competitor makes. Authorship is the actual difference.

   - BEFORE: "Where AI fits your sprint and where it stops: refinement, acceptance criteria, ordering, and why Copilot cannot read your Jira. Free 21-page guide."
   - AFTER: "Copilot probably can't read your Jira. What AI can still do for refinement and acceptance criteria, and what stays yours. Free 21-page guide."
   - WHY: opens on the recognition moment; same keywords, 146 chars.
