Workflow automation is now a standard tool for European businesses. The question is less whether to automate than which platform to use. For European companies that care about data location, GDPR and vendor independence, the deciding factor is often where the platform processes your data. This article compares n8n, Zapier and Make on that question first, then on features, pricing models and self-hosting.

## Short Answer: Is n8n or Zapier Better for GDPR?

No tool is GDPR-compliant by itself. What matters is where your workflow data is processed, who can access it, and what contracts are in place. On data location, the three platforms differ, based on each vendor's own documentation (checked on 20 November 2026):

| | Zapier | Make | n8n (self-hosted) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Where data is stored** | AWS in the United States ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/legal/data-privacy)) | US or EU region, chosen per organization ([Make](https://help.make.com/organizations)) | A server you choose, for example in Germany |
| **Transfer mechanism to the US** | EU-US Data Privacy Framework, SCCs as fallback ([Zapier DPA](https://zapier.com/legal/data-processing-addendum)) | Not needed for storage in the EU region; check sub-processors | Not needed if your server and services are in the EU |
| **Who operates the infrastructure** | Zapier and its sub-processors | Make and its sub-processors | You, or the hosting provider you choose |
| **What you still need to do** | DPA, records, your own decision on US processing | DPA, check of sub-processors | DPA with your hosting provider, security and operations |

If your workflows process personal data and you want it to stay in the EU, self-hosted n8n gives you the most control over location, because you choose the server. Zapier and Make can be used under GDPR too, with the contracts and checks listed above. For the legal background, see our [GDPR-compliant AI guide](/resources/blog/gdpr-compliant-ai-guide/). Which option is acceptable for your data is a decision for your data protection officer or legal advisers.

## The Three Contenders

**Zapier** is one of the best-known automation platforms. Founded in 2011, it offers a very large catalogue of app integrations and a simple trigger-action interface. It is a US company and states that it stores data in the United States.

**Make** (formerly Integromat) is a visual workflow builder founded in the Czech Republic. It offers a drag-and-drop scenario editor with routers, iterators and error handlers. Customers choose a US or EU region for their data.

**n8n** is a workflow tool founded in Berlin. Its source code is publicly available under n8n's own "fair-code" license, and you can run it on your own servers. It offers a visual workflow editor, native integrations, and the ability to write custom code within workflows. Check n8n's license terms for your intended use.

## Feature Comparison

### Workflow Complexity

Zapier's trigger-action model is quick to set up for simple automations: "When I receive an email with an attachment, save it to Google Drive." It also supports branching and multi-step workflows; check its current feature list for the logic you need.

Make's visual builder supports parallel branches, iterators, routers, and error handlers. Workflows with several branches can be built as a single Make scenario.

n8n offers comparable workflow logic and adds the ability to write custom JavaScript or Python code directly within workflow nodes. This helps with data transformation, API calls with custom authentication, or integration with internal systems that lack pre-built connectors. You can also create custom nodes and share them across your organization.

### Integration Breadth

Zapier has the largest catalogue of pre-built integrations, Make a large one, and n8n fewer native integrations. Check each vendor's integration directory for your specific tools.

Numbers tell an incomplete story. Most businesses use 10-20 tools actively. The question is whether your specific tools are supported. All three platforms cover the most common business applications (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot, databases). Where they differ is in long-tail integrations with niche industry software.

n8n compensates for fewer native integrations with its HTTP Request node (for any API), its ability to run custom code, and custom nodes. For internal systems, this flexibility can matter more than a large library of pre-built connectors.

### AI Integration

All three platforms now offer AI capabilities, but the approaches differ:

Zapier and Make offer AI steps through integrations with providers such as OpenAI and Anthropic. Data sent in these steps goes to the AI provider's API.

n8n offers similar integrations, and when self-hosted you can also connect it to AI models running on your own infrastructure. Then your workflows can use AI for document processing, classification, summarization, and generation without sending the data to an external AI provider.

## Pricing Models

The pricing models differ and can change the cost picture at scale. Prices change often, so check each vendor's current pricing page before deciding:

**Zapier** charges per "task": each action step in a workflow run counts as one task. A workflow that runs 50 times a day with 10 action steps uses about 15,000 tasks per month.

**Make** charges by usage units per scenario run (historically called "operations"), with plans by monthly volume.

**n8n** offers a cloud version with plans by number of executions, and a self-hosted version. Self-hosting the Community Edition has no license fee for use within n8n's license terms; you pay for the server and your own operations time. Features such as SSO and advanced permissions are in n8n's paid plans.

For high volumes, compare the cost of a usage-based plan with the fixed cost of a self-hosted server plus operations. Which is cheaper depends on your volumes and on how much operations work you can do in-house.

## Data Location in Detail

For European companies that process personal data in their workflows, data location is often the deciding question.

### Zapier

Zapier is a US company. It states that it hosts data on AWS servers in the United States and does not offer EU-only storage ([Zapier: Data Privacy Overview](https://zapier.com/legal/data-privacy)). For transfers of European data, Zapier relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, with Standard Contractual Clauses in its data processing addendum as a fallback ([Zapier DPA](https://zapier.com/legal/data-processing-addendum)). The EU General Court upheld the Data Privacy Framework in September 2025; an appeal is pending at the Court of Justice. More on this in our [GDPR-compliant AI guide](/resources/blog/gdpr-compliant-ai-guide/).

If your workflows process personal data (customer emails, employee information, order details), you need a data processing agreement, a record of the processing, and your own decision whether processing in the US is acceptable for that data.

### Make

Make is a European company. Each organization chooses a US or EU region when it is created, and the region determines where its data is stored and processed ([Make Help Center](https://help.make.com/organizations)). According to Make's sub-processor list, hosting runs on AWS. Check Make's current sub-processor list and DPA for the services that apply to your plan.

### n8n

Self-hosting is n8n's main difference. When you run n8n on your own server, on-premises or at a European hosting provider such as Hetzner, you decide where workflows, credentials and execution logs are stored. If you use a hosting provider, that provider is a processor, so you need a DPA with it as well.

For companies in regulated industries (finance, healthcare, legal) or companies handling sensitive personal data, this control over location can make the compliance assessment considerably simpler.

## Self-Hosting: What It Actually Takes

The concern many businesses have about self-hosting is operational complexity. Here is what self-hosting n8n typically requires:

**Infrastructure:** A Linux server with 2+ CPU cores and 4+ GB RAM. For production use, add a PostgreSQL database and regular backups. Check current server prices with your hosting provider.

**Deployment:** n8n provides official Docker images. With Docker Compose, you can define your entire stack (n8n, database, reverse proxy) in a single configuration file.

**Maintenance:** n8n releases updates regularly. Updating a Docker-based deployment is typically a single command. Database backups should be automated.

**Monitoring:** Basic health monitoring can be set up with free tools. n8n exposes health check endpoints that integrate with standard monitoring solutions.

For companies with DevOps capability, this is manageable. For companies without technical staff, a managed n8n setup on European infrastructure (which Ironum also offers) takes over the operations while you keep control of the location.

## Example Use Cases

The following are typical scenarios, not specific client projects.

### Invoice Processing

A [manufacturing](/industries/manufacturing/) company receives invoices by email. An n8n workflow extracts key data with a locally hosted AI model, validates it against the ERP system, routes it for approval based on amount thresholds, and posts approved invoices to the accounting software. All data stays on the company's own or EU-hosted infrastructure.

### Customer Onboarding

A software company automates customer onboarding: CRM update, welcome email sequence, a Slack notification to the customer success team, and account provisioning in its product, all in one workflow.

### Compliance Reporting

A financial services firm generates weekly compliance reports by aggregating data from several internal systems, running checks, formatting the reports, and sending them to the relevant people. The financial data stays on its private infrastructure.

## When to Choose Each Platform

**Zapier can fit if** you need a very large integration catalogue, your workflows are simple, you process little or no personal data in them, or you have assessed and accepted processing in the US, and you want no infrastructure to maintain.

**Make can fit if** you want a visual builder for complex scenarios, a European vendor, and data storage in an EU region, without running your own servers.

**n8n can fit if** you want to decide where your data is stored, want to use AI models on your own infrastructure, process sensitive or personal data, want to avoid usage-based pricing, or need custom integrations with internal systems.

For European businesses that handle personal data or work in regulated industries, n8n's self-hosting option is a strong argument, because it puts the location of the data in your hands.
